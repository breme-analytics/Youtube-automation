# Retention: Mario Joos's principles

Mario Joos was MrBeast's retention director (2020 to 2023) and runs Brightrock, a research and
consulting company for large YouTube channels. Paraphrased from his own words in:
- Interview, Net Influencer, 16 June 2025 ("Mario Joos Breaks Down The Science Behind YouTube Success")
- His newsletter, newsletter.mariojoos.com: September, October and November 2024 issues
- His VidSummit 2026 talk, as reported by AIR Media-Tech
- His LinkedIn posts "10 tips to improve audience retention" and "10 tips to keep attention" (2024),
  read 12 Oct 2026

## His principles

1. **The whole video matters.** Most advice stops at the title, thumbnail and intro; the rest of the
   video is "the food". Minute seven deserves the care second seven gets.
2. **Progressive pacing.** Every sentence must move the video forward to the next one. Tight, but
   not so fast that it overloads the viewer.
3. **No needless repetition.** Don't summarise what you just explained; the viewer can rewind.
4. **Make it visual.** Change what's on screen; scenery changes, storytelling and (where it fits)
   humour keep people watching. Quick cuts alone don't guarantee retention.
5. **Shot length follows complexity.** A simple shot needs about half a second to a second; a
   complex one one to three seconds to be understood without overload.
6. **Vary the reactions.** Avoid stalling by mixing seven kinds of reaction to an idea: emotional,
   analytical, descriptive, conclusive, contextual, elevating and comedic.
7. **Talk plainly.** You don't need to act or speak fancy to tell a story; it can feel inauthentic.
8. **Music supports, never overpowers, the voice.**
9. **Peak-end rule.** People judge an experience by its peak and its end. Build to a peak and end
   strong.
10. **Enjoyment keeps people watching; satisfaction brings them back.** Deliver what the title promised.
11. **Don't give people a video they didn't click for.** Confirm the title's promise early.
12. **Attention spans haven't collapsed; people judge faster.** Never waste their time.
13. **Validate ideas with outliers:** videos that beat their channel's usual views show what people want.
14. **Length follows engagement:** as long as the idea stays interesting, no longer.
15. **No magic numbers.** There's no CTR or retention percentage to hit; compare retention-graph
    patterns and whether a change raised total views.
16. **Use silence to create tension.** A short moment of silence before something important builds
    suspense and makes viewers lean in.
17. **Add strong emotion to voice lines that signal key moments.** Excitement or fear tells the viewer
    something big is coming.
18. **Keep the pacing dynamic.** Speed up through information; slow down when building anticipation.
19. **Skip the announcements; jump right in.** Don't tell people what's coming, show it.
20. **Voiceover moves the story forward.** It never just describes what's already on screen.
21. **Be specific.** Avoid vague "this", "there", "that"; name the thing.
22. **Use visual contrast for key information:** bold text, colour or highlights on the important words.
23. **Avoid needless zooms and camera moves.** Every movement needs a purpose.
24. **Break complex ideas into small parts.**
25. **Put the outcome first and the action last** in a sentence: "To change your life, change one thought."
26. **Keep transitions short and clear.**
27. **Visuals beat text,** as long as they're clear and look good.

## How this channel applies them

- **Hook (first 20 to 30 seconds):** open on the stakes the title raised, confirm the promise, and open
  one question (the "open loop") that only the final chapter answers. No greeting, no "in this video",
  no channel intro.
- **Cliffhangers:** every chapter except the last ends on a turn, a question or a reveal that the next
  chapter delivers.
- **Silence:** "[pause]" before 3 to 6 reveals holds a beat of silence in George's narration (principle 16).
- **Chapters:** each one moves through the idea, the old book's words, what modern psychology found, and
  what it means for the viewer.
- **No recaps.** A one-line callback is fine; a summary isn't.
- **Pictures match the words:** each scene shows what its sentence means. A free vision model checks every
  stock clip against the narration and rejects any that distracts; if no clip fits, a painting of the
  moment is used instead. Camera movement stays slow and purposeful (principle 23).
- **Captions:** the spoken phrase appears at the bottom with the current word in gold (principle 22;
  many people watch with the sound off). They hide while a quote card or chapter title is on screen.
- **Ending:** the strongest insight comes last (the peak) and answers the open question, then one
  concrete practice, then stop within about 15 seconds. No long outro.
